The Rising Cost of Judgment

AI has made answers almost free. What keeps getting more expensive is knowing where a person has to step in.

When the spreadsheet arrived in the early 1980s, accountants did not disappear. They nearly doubled, because the work moved from arithmetic to deciding what the numbers meant. AI is doing the same to the first draft. This essay asks the question that comes next: before automating anything, how much does it cost to get it wrong, and where do we need to be to prevent it?

What's inside

  • The Vernuli Matrix: break a process into concrete actions and ask each one three questions: what kind of solution does it call for, how much does a mistake cost, and how much does reviewing cost. See the Matrix →
  • The three conditions of judgment: a person in the loop only protects anything if they have a source the AI doesn't have, the time to use it, and the authority to stop.
  • Public cases where review sat in the wrong place, and one built from scratch: an AI video production line where moving review from the finished animation to the storyboard cut the cost of a fix from about $7 to $1.20.
